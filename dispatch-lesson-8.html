Two Places, One Team
Two Places, One Team
Story 1 (Lesson 8)

Anna used to spend every weekday at her desk in the city, but under the new hybrid policy she only had to commute to the office three days a week and could work from home on the other two. She had recently moved into a house in the country, halfway between Warsaw and Radom, and being able to work from home twice a week turned out to be a perk she valued more than she had expected.

At work, the working environment had shifted considerably since the policy changed. Anna's team occasionally worked with a partner office in Rotterdam, and she noticed that the Dutch rarely stayed late; in fact, most Dutch people she had met seemed surprised that some of her Warsaw colleagues still regularly worked long hours. Anna hoped all these changes at her own company would eventually bring Warsaw closer to that balance.

The following Monday, the accounts receivable team flagged a discrepancy with accounts payable: a supplier invoice had appeared twice in the system. Anna asked one of her analysts to explain the situation to her, and once he had explained to her the difference between the duplicate entry and the original invoice, the correction took only a few minutes. She later told the finance director that the mismatch had happened because a batch upload had failed halfway through the process, not because of any error on the supplier's side.

Explaining the Numbers to the US Office
Explaining the Numbers to the US Office

Ahead of the call with the US office, Anna prepared a short summary in plain language rather than technical jargon, since not everyone on the call worked in finance. She added a little illustrative language to help explain the trend more clearly. The summary noted that overdue invoices had fallen by roughly fifteen percent, that the total outstanding balance was now approximately two million euros, and that the review process had taken about three weeks. One slide referred to an older system introduced circa 1995, long before the current one.

During the call, Anna explained how the reconciliation process worked in the US compared with Poland. One of the American colleagues mentioned that the US population's growing use of digital payments had changed reporting requirements over the past decade, and that a recent shift in US foreign policy had indirectly disrupted a few of their international suppliers. He added, half-joking, that a recent case before the US Supreme Court had briefly confused the compliance team, and that a statement from the US president earlier that year had done much the same.

After the call, Anna found an ad hoc task waiting on her desk: a reconciliation query that needed an answer the same day. She decided to postpone the less urgent items until Monday and focus on using her time to best effect for the rest of the afternoon. Even so, she ended up with some work to do after work that evening. She later reminded a junior colleague not to fetishise long hours as proof of commitment – finishing the right tasks mattered far more than simply staying late.
