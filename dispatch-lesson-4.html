Lesson 4 – Vocabulary

🟠 Orders and Tasks
to process an order
realizować / obsługiwać zamówienie
Our sales team usually processes online orders within two working days.
to carry out a task
realizować / wykonywać zadanie
The finance team is carrying out several important checks this week.
to implement a plan
realizować / wdrażać plan
We are now implementing the changes agreed during the last meeting.
to complete a project
zrealizować / ukończyć projekt
We expect to complete the project by the end of September.
to achieve a goal
zrealizować / osiągnąć cel
The department achieved all its annual objectives.
to fulfil an obligation
zrealizować / wypełnić zobowiązanie
Both parties must fulfil their contractual obligations.
Polish realizować does not have one universal English equivalent. The correct verb depends on the context.
🟡 International Teamwork
to get to know each other better
lepiej się poznać
The informal dinner gave us an opportunity to get to know each other better.
a team-building event
wydarzenie integracyjne; ogólne określenie, nie precyzuje miejsca ani czasu trwania
The company is organising a team-building event for employees from both offices.
a team-building day
jednodniowe wydarzenie integracyjne
We are planning a team-building day before the new team starts working together.
a team-building retreat
wyjazd integracyjny; zwykle poza miejscem pracy, często z noclegiem
The Polish and Indian teams are going on a team-building retreat together.
an offsite
wydarzenie służbowe poza zwykłym miejscem pracy, może łączyć spotkania, szkolenia i integrację
They are organising an offsite that combines training sessions with social activities.
The noun offsite is common in everyday corporate English. In more formal writing, prefer an off-site meeting, an off-site event, or an event held away from the office.
A team-building event is a general term and does not specify the location or duration. A team-building day is a one-day event. A team-building retreat usually involves travelling away from the workplace and often staying overnight. An offsite is an organised work event held away from the usual workplace and may combine meetings, training and social activities.
differences between A and B
różnice między A a B
There are a lot of differences between our working styles.
even if someone does not understand a thing
nawet jeśli ktoś nic nie rozumie
People sometimes remain silent even if they do not understand a thing.
🟢 Training and Future Plans
to go back to a country
wrócić do kraju
At the end of July, they are going back to India.
to train other people
szkolić inne osoby
After returning home, they will train other employees.
🟣 Making Notes
to put something down
zapisać coś
Let me put your contact details down before I forget them.
In this context, put something down means write something down. However, write something down is usually clearer, because put down has several other meanings.
to note something down
zanotować coś
I noted down the main decisions made during the meeting.
to jot something down
szybko coś zanotować
I need a moment to jot down this telephone number.
To jot something down is slightly more informal and suggests writing something quickly.
🔵 Skills and Achievements
to be good at something
być dobrym w czymś
One of our colleagues from India is very good at cricket.
to win an award
zdobyć nagrodę
He has won several awards during his sporting career.
🟠 Time and Dates
until the end of this week
do końca tego tygodnia
The visitors will work in our office until the end of this week.
the week after
tydzień później; w kolejnym tygodniu
I am busy next week, but I am available the week after.
The week after refers to the week following another week already mentioned.
for a month
przez miesiąc
They are staying in Poland for a month.
on 15 July / on the 15th of July
15 lipca
The next meeting will take place on 15 July.
In speech, say on July fifteenth or on July the fifteenth.
🟡 Recovery
to recover
dojść do siebie; wyzdrowieć; odzyskać siły
She needs a few more days to recover after the operation.
🟢 Studies Abroad
to study in the Netherlands
studiować w Holandii
My daughter is studying in the Netherlands.
to study for a bachelor's degree
studiować na studiach licencjackich
She is studying for a bachelor's degree in international business.
✅ She is studying for a bachelor's degree.
🚫 She is studying for bachelor's degree.
Use an apostrophe in bachelor's, and use the article a: a bachelor's degree. When you name the subject, use a degree in + subject: a degree in international business.
🟣 Countries and Organisations
the United States / the US
Stany Zjednoczone
Our company has several clients in the United States.
the United Kingdom / the UK
Zjednoczone Królestwo
The head office is in the UK.
the European Union / the EU
Unia Europejska
The new regulations apply throughout the EU.
The Hague
Haga
Several international courts are based in The Hague.
Remember that the full name is The Hague.
🔵 Nationalities
the Dutch
Holendrzy jako grupa narodowa
The survey compared attitudes among the Dutch, the Germans and the Belgians.
Dutch people
Holendrzy; osoby z Holandii
She works with several Dutch people in the finance department.
The Dutch is a collective expression for Dutch people as a national group. Dutch people is more natural when referring to particular individuals or an unspecified number of people.
🟠 Family
to miss your family
tęsknić za rodziną
She misses her family a lot while she is studying abroad.
🟡 Useful Correction
✅ Half of my activities involve working with international teams.
🚫 A half of my activities involve working with international teams.
Half of is usually the most natural form. A half of is possible when the half is treated as one distinct portion: He gave me a half of the sandwich and kept the other half for himself. In this sentence, half of my activities is the natural form.
Do not confuse this with half a / half an, used before a singular noun with no of: half a day, half an hour. 🚫 a half of hour
🟢 Lesson Arrangements
to make a payment
dokonać płatności
I will make the payment for the next four lessons this week.

Before the retreat could begin, there was still work to carry out. Anna's team needed to process a backlog of orders left over from the previous month, implement a new approval step agreed at the last review, and complete the project before the visit. Both sides had also promised the board that they would fulfil their reporting obligations on time, and the next report was due on 15 July, only a few days after the retreat ended. By June, Anna's team had achieved every goal set for the first half of the year, and the board wanted the two offices to get to know each other better before the busiest quarter began. Rather than organising another single team-building day in the Warsaw office, management decided on a team-building retreat: both the Polish and the Indian accounts receivable teams would leave their usual offices and spend several days together at a conference centre outside the city. The programme was structured as an offsite, combining training sessions each morning with informal activities in the afternoons, and the Indian visitors were staying in Poland for a month in total, since the retreat was only the first part of a longer visit.

During the sessions, Anna noticed differences between the two teams' working styles, particularly in their approach to documentation, though everyone had agreed beforehand that it was fine to ask again even if someone did not understand a thing the first time it was explained. One of the visiting colleagues turned out to be very good at cricket and had already won several awards during his sporting career. By the second evening, the others were joking that no one should agree to play against him. Before going back to India at the end of the visit, the group agreed that the Indian team would train other employees once they returned home. Anna spent the last afternoon making sure everything important was written down: she put down the contact details of each visiting colleague, noted down the follow-up actions from the training sessions, and asked a junior colleague to jot down the names of two systems that still needed to be aligned. In her closing email to both teams, she wrote plainly: "The alignment work will not begin until the end of this week at the earliest and may have to wait until the week after."

Not every part of Anna's week involved the visiting team. One of her colleagues, Marta, was on a work placement in Rotterdam. She had been living in the Netherlands for almost a year and was studying for a bachelor's degree in international business alongside the placement. During a catch-up call, Marta admitted that she still missed her family whenever her flat felt too quiet in the evenings, though she had settled in well and worked closely with Dutch people in the office every day. She found their communication style direct, but once she became familiar with it, she found them easy to work with. Anna sometimes joked that half of her own working week now involved international contacts of one kind or another, whether in the United States, the United Kingdom, or closer to home.

One of Anna's regular contacts worked for a client based near The Hague, coordinating a shared project that also touched several partners across the European Union. In an email that week, the client mentioned needing a few extra days to recover after a minor operation, which delayed one report slightly, but nothing serious was affected. Looking back over her week, Anna realised that half of her activities now involved some form of international coordination.
