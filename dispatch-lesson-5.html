A Change of Hotel
A Change of Hotel
Story 1 · Lesson 5

Anna travelled to Spała for a two-day training session with colleagues from the regional office and several business partners. The first hotel had no air conditioning, and with no other room available at the hotel, the group decided they needed to change hotels. After checking two alternatives nearby, Anna and her colleague agreed to choose a more luxurious hotel closer to the training centre, and the group moved to another hotel just before evening. The new hotel had far better facilities: a small business centre where guests could print documents, a gym and pool, and several shops nearby.

On the first evening, Anna and her business partners had dinner together. The schedule for the following two days was tight, so everyone agreed to have breakfast early. After the long journey, Anna decided to have a rest before dinner, and by the second evening the group had settled into a routine that left enough time to take a rest between sessions.

Figures and Follow-Ups
Figures and Follow-Ups
Story 2 · Lesson 5
Loading…

Anna works as an accountant in Accounts, where being good at figures matters every day. The company is active in international trade, selling through wholesale channels to retailers across the region, though a small part of the business still involves domestic trade closer to home. The report Anna was preparing that week focused mainly on foreign trade, and the company had also seen steady growth in online commerce over the past year. One afternoon, a colleague from the claims-handling department asked whether Anna's team could help with a case involving one of their long-standing business partners and several overdue payments.

Anna opened the overdue payments file and reviewed the figures: the first column listed each debtor's name, the second the amount owed. One client owed the company a large amount of money, and Anna made a note to contact them again soon. In these cases, the company was the creditor and the client was the debtor. The company always contacted clients whose accounts were more than thirty days overdue. In her reply, Anna wrote: "Thank you for your email of 12 February." She also confirmed that the updated report would be ready on 15 July. The company owned several office buildings across the region, a reminder that owing money and owning property were two very different things in the department's daily work.
